**Mayflower voyage clip (`s5-pilgrim-voyage.mp4`):** the opening departure from Plymouth (September 6, 1620) and the Mayflower Compact cabin scene (November 11, 1620) are AI-generated, animated from generated period stills (Separatist-era dress: doublets, falling collars, coifs, capotains, no buckles), and were checked frame by frame. Seven animation takes were made for each scene and only the cleanest was kept. On-screen fact cards use [EB Garamond](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/EB+Garamond) and [IM Fell English SC](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/IM+Fell+English+SC) (both SIL Open Font License 1.1).

**Early Virginia clip (`s5-virginia-struggles.mp4`):** the tobacco-field footage is unchanged; it now carries on-screen fact cards timed to the narration (1607–1610 hardships, John Smith's 1608 rule, the Starving Time of 1609–1610, Rolfe's tobacco of 1612, the House of Burgesses on July 30, 1619, and the first recorded Africans in August 1619), in the same fonts as the Mayflower cards.

**New England clip (`s5-new-england.mp4`):** six scenes, one per caption: the first winter (still, slow push-in), the March 1621 treaty of Massasoit and Governor Carver, Tisquantum planting corn with fish, the autumn 1621 harvest gathering, the 1630 Winthrop fleet (still), and a New England town street (still). The treaty, planting, and harvest scenes are AI-generated animations of generated period stills (Wampanoag deerskin, Separatist-era English dress), six takes each, checked frame by frame, with the cleanest kept. Fact cards carry the dates and numbers.

**Beringia clip (`s1-beringia.mp4`):** the sea-level scenes are the NOAA [PALE Paleoenvironmental Atlas of Beringia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Beringia_land_bridge-noaagov.gif) coastline maps (NOAA, public domain), shown at 21,000 years ago and then animated from 13,000 years ago to 11,000 years ago and on to today, with added labels, legend, and year counter. The hunters on the steppe and the skin boats on the coast are AI-generated animations of generated stills (six takes each, checked frame by frame, the cleanest kept). The coastal route is shown as a theory, which is how scientists treat it.

**Early maize clip (`s1-early-maize.mp4`):** the teosinte, hybrid, and maize ears are a real photograph, [Maize-teosinte.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Maize-teosinte.jpg) by John Doebley ([CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0)), placed on a matching red background. The Ice Age steppe, the Balsas valley rock shelter, the Three Sisters field, and the farming village are AI-generated animations of generated stills (six takes each for the new scenes, checked frame by frame, the cleanest kept; the steppe shot is an unused take from the Beringia set). The Three Sisters field was regenerated so it shows only period tools: a hoe with a deer shoulder-blade (bone) blade, a plain wooden digging stick, and a hoe with a chipped stone blade, each lashed to a wooden handle, with no metal. Corn, beans, and squash grow together on hills, and the houses are bark-covered Eastern Woodlands dome houses (wigwams).

**Clovis points clip (`s1-clovis-points.mp4`):** five scenes, one per caption: the 1930s dig at Blackwater Draw with mammoth bones, the point turning on hide (shown forward, then in reverse), a point being lashed to a spear shaft by a fire, and hunters near mammoths at a water hole. All are AI-generated animations, six takes each for the dig, hafting, and hunt scenes, checked frame by frame, with the cleanest kept. Fact cards carry the dates, stones, and finds.

Every key also has captions, Key Facts, and a check in `content/videos.js`. They work with or without the file.

**Paleo-Indians clip (`s1-paleo-indians.mp4`):** five scenes, one per caption: a band of hunters in hide clothing crossing an Ice Age plain with mammoths in the distance, women and children gathering plants, archaeologists brushing a stone point out of the ground, a hide-covered shelter by a forest creek at Monte Verde, Chile, and an adult and child leaving footprints on a wet lakeshore at White Sands, New Mexico. All are AI-generated animations of generated stills, checked frame by frame; takes with doubled spears, an extra digging stick, an extra stone point or brush, or a person who changes mid-shot were rejected. Monte Verde is described as "older than Clovis", and the White Sands dates as still being tested, which is where the science stands.

**Olmec, Maya, and Aztec clip (`s1-olmec-maya-aztec.mp4`):** the glyph page is a real photograph of page 9 of the Maya [Dresden Codex](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dresden_Codex_p09.jpg) (public domain), shown with a slow zoom instead of AI-drawn glyphs. The Olmec stone head, Tikal, Tenochtitlán on Lake Texcoco, and the chinampas are AI-generated animations of generated stills, checked frame by frame. Stills were regenerated where they went wrong (a Maya-style pyramid behind the Aztec chinampas; a too-perfectly round Tenochtitlán).

**Inca clip (`s1-inca-empire.mp4`):** the terraces scene is a real photograph, [Machu Picchu, Peru.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Machu_Picchu,_Peru.jpg) by Pedro Szekely ([CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0)), shown with a slow zoom. Cusco, the rope bridge with llamas, the chasqui runner with a quipu, and the mit'a work crew are AI-generated animations of generated stills, checked frame by frame. Stills and takes were rejected for things that came only after the Spanish conquest: knitted chullo caps and red Spanish-style tile roofs.

**Mound Builders clip (`s1-mound-builders.mp4`):** five scenes, one per caption: workers carrying baskets of earth up a mound, Cahokia and Monks Mound around 1100 (before its log wall was built), Hohokam irrigation canals in the Arizona desert, Pueblo Bonito at Chaco Canyon, and Cliff Palace at Mesa Verde (with turkeys, which Ancestral Puebloans raised). All are AI-generated animations of generated stills, checked frame by frame.

The four Level 1 clips above use the same narrator voice, ambience beds, and fact-card fonts as the other clips.

**Level 2 clips (Section 2, six scrolls):** `s2-culture-regions.mp4`, `s2-pacific-northwest.mp4`, `s2-great-plains.mp4`, `s2-southwest.mp4`, `s2-iroquois.mp4`, and `s2-southeast.mp4`, five scenes each, one per caption. All scenes are AI-generated animations of generated stills, at least two takes for scenes with people or animals moving, checked frame by frame, with the cleanest kept. Takes were rejected for: a modern steel hatchet (Northwest carver; replaced by an adze-like carving tool), glass-paned windows in a plank house, a sail on an Eastern Woodlands canoe, a metal-looking shovel in a Pueblo field, cloned identical people on a pueblo roof, and props that popped in and out. A Southeast council-house still was regenerated because the people and dress did not look like Southeastern Native nations. Accuracy choices: Plains scenes before the horse show hunting on foot (under wolf skins) and dog travois, and the horse scene is carded "1600s–1700s"; the Northwest cards say tall totem poles came mostly in the 1800s; Pueblo walls are stone with adobe plaster (no molded bricks before the Spanish); Navajo sheep and wool weaving are labeled as after the Spanish; the wampum belt follows the Hiawatha belt design; the Iroquois council shot is cropped to keep a skylight-like smoke hole out of frame.

The Mound Builders clip was rebuilt in the Level 2 update with a new Cahokia shot (an earlier take showed smoke rising from the tall post).
